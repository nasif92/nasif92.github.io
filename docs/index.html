01 / Research

From nuclei to clinical scores

01.1Cell classificationReading individual nuclei

I benchmarked StarDist and Cellpose for nucleus detection and trained a classifier against pathologist annotations. It uses measurements a reviewer can inspect: a nucleus’s shape, staining intensity and relationship to nearby cells.

My next question is what image texture adds to those measurements. I’m investigating how to combine learned representations with handcrafted features across stains and magnifications, particularly when only a small fraction of nuclei have expert labels.

01.2Open-source softwareScoring slides in QuPath

I built tools that bring segmentation outputs into QuPath and support two scoring workflows: PD-L1 Combined Positive Score and Magee equation calculations. Keeping the detections on the slide lets a pathologist inspect the cells behind a score.

Explore my QuPath fork ↗
01.3Interpretable modellingTesting the stability of Magee triage

When two versions of a pipeline give different results, which changes actually matter? I study how variations in ER, PR and Ki-67 measurements affect Magee-based triage for Oncotype DX testing.

I’m exploring decision trees as a way to show where a change in a biomarker measurement might change a triage decision. This is a research tool, not a clinical recommendation.

When Does Unlabelled Data Help? A Shift-Aware Semi-supervised Study of Multimodal Mitral Valve Segmentation

Sidratul Montaha, Nasif Hossain, Michelle Noga, Kumaradevan Punithakumar, Nilanjan Ray

We studied CT and surgical video alongside a fully supervised ultrasound reference. Reliable pseudo-labels helped in both CT and video, but methods designed to handle changes in image appearance mattered most for surgical video.
